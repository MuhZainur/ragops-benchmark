## ITEM 1. BUSINESS

NVIDIA pioneered accelerated computing to tackle challenges ordinary computers cannot. Since our founding in 1993, we have specialized in visual computing and accelerated computing architectures. We invented the Graphics Processing Unit (GPU) in 1999, which defined modern computer graphics, sparked PC gaming, and ignited the modern era of Artificial Intelligence (AI).

### Platform Architecture & Operating Segments
Our operations are organized into two primary reportable segments:
1. **Compute & Networking:** Includes our Data Center accelerated computing platforms, quantum computing architectures, networking infrastructure (Quantum InfiniBand, Spectrum Ethernet, BlueField DPUs), AI Enterprise software, and automotive autonomous driving platforms (NVIDIA DRIVE).
2. **Graphics:** Includes GeForce GPUs for gaming and PCs, GeForce NOW cloud gaming, Quadro/NVIDIA RTX GPUs for enterprise workstation graphics, and virtual GPU software for cloud-based visual computing.

### Manufacturing & Supply Chain
NVIDIA utilizes a fabless manufacturing strategy. We partner with world-class independent semiconductor foundries, primarily Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing Company Limited (TSMC), for wafer manufacturing, as well as specialized subcontractors in Asia for packaging, assembly, and testing.

## ITEM 1A. RISK FACTORS

### Supply Chain & Manufacturing Concentration
We rely on third-party foundries, primarily TSMC, to manufacture all of our semiconductor wafers. We do not own or operate wafer fabrication facilities. If TSMC experiences disruptions due to natural disasters, geopolitical tensions in Taiwan, equipment failures, or capacity constraints, our ability to supply accelerated computing products would be severely impaired.

### Export Controls and Geopolitics
In October 2023, the U.S. Bureau of Industry and Security (BIS) updated export regulations concerning advanced computing chips and semiconductor manufacturing equipment destined for China and other restricted jurisdictions. These regulations expanded licensing requirements for products exceeding specific performance thresholds (including our A100, H100, A800, and H800 architectures). Historically, sales to customers headquartered in China represented 20% to 25% of our Data Center revenue. While we have developed alternative compliant products for the Chinese market, future revisions to U.S. export policies could permanently reduce our addressable market.

### Segment Performance Discussion
* **Compute & Networking:** Revenue surged 217% to $47,405 million. Growth was overwhelmingly driven by unprecedented enterprise and cloud service provider demand for NVIDIA HGX platforms based on the Hopper architecture (H100 and H200 Tensor Core GPUs) powering generative AI model training and inferencing.
* **Graphics:** Revenue grew 14% to $13,517 million, reflecting normalized channel inventory and strong retail demand for GeForce RTX 40 Series GPUs.
* **Gross Margin:** Expanded significantly from 56.9% to 72.7%, reflecting high component operating leverage and a favorable sales mix heavily weighted toward high-margin Data Center architectures.
